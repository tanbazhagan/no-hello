# No Hello: How to Start a Chat Message

Don't send a greeting and then wait for a reply. Assume the other person will respond, and write your full message right away. Whenever they pick it up, they can act on it without a back-and-forth. See [Credits](#credits) for the sources.

## The Idea

Starting with "hi" or "are you there?" makes you wait for a reply before you say anything useful. Skip that step. Start typing what you need, and send it as one message. They read it when they're free and answer once, instead of replying to a greeting first and then asking what you want.

Being friendly and being direct go together. Put the greeting and the message in the same note.

## Why It Works

- **They can start thinking right away.** While you're still typing your question after a "Hi", they're sitting idle. Typing is slower than talking, so the pause costs more in chat than in person.
- **They don't have to guess.** A bare "Hello" could be a social note, a check-in, or an urgent request. Say which.
- **It works when they're away.** If they step out, they come back to a question they can answer, not a lone "Hello" and a puzzle about what they missed.
- **It's respectful.** Busy people multitask, and saving their time is the polite thing to do.

## Rules

1. **Don't wait for a reply to a greeting.** Messages like "hi", "hello", "you around?", "quick question", "ping", and "yt?" only start a wait. Write what you need instead.
2. **Say what you need in the first sentence.** A greeting is fine if it sits in the same message as the ask.
3. **Don't ask for permission to ask.** "Can I ask you something?" and "Do you have a minute?" only trigger a "sure" and another wait. Ask the question.
4. **Don't open with small talk that expects a reply.** "How are you?" gets a "good, you?" and nothing else. Fold the warmth into a real message, such as "Hope your week is going well! Quick question: ..."
5. **Include the context and the deadline.** Add the links, numbers, or options the person needs to answer.
6. **Write so they can reply without asking you anything first.** Assume they'll read it later, between meetings.
7. **Don't wait for a reply before you keep going.** If you can make progress while you wait, do it, and say what you'll do in the meantime.
8. **Switch to a call** if you expect more than three back-and-forth replies.
9. **In a group conversation, @mention me if you need immediate attention.** Don't type my name alone. Put the ask in the same message as the mention.
   - ❌ "@John"
   - ✅ "@John, can you approve the UAT deploy by 3 PM? The release is blocked on it."

## Examples

❌ "hi" ... (waits) ... "you there?" ... (waits) ... "what time was that meeting?"

❌ "Hey Ravi, got a minute?" (They'll say yes, and then you start typing. Start typing first.)

❌ "Hi Meera, how's your day going?" (You'll get "good, you?" and nothing more.)

❌ "Hello Daniel." (A name and a greeting with no ask.)

**Asking for something**

✅ "Hi Tim! What time is the client review on Friday? I'm blocking my calendar."

✅ "Hi Priya, I need a go or no-go on the Azure Front Door option by end of day. CDN costs are rising 30% a quarter. Option A keeps the current setup; Option B adds edge caching. I recommend B. Can you confirm? If I don't hear back, I'll start on B tomorrow morning."

✅ "Hi Sofia, hope your week is going well! Do you still have the link to the sizing spreadsheet you shared last Thursday? I need it for tomorrow's estimate review."

**Following up**

✅ "Hi Marcus, thanks for walking me through the API gateway design yesterday. Have you had a chance to review the questions I sent? I'd like to close them out before Friday's sprint planning."

**Introductions**

✅ "Hi Lena, I'm on the platform team. Carlos suggested you're the right person for questions about the notification service. Could we find 30 minutes next week for me to ask a few?"

**Just saying hi**

✅ "Hi Omar, it's been months since the offsite! I wanted to catch up and hear how the new project is going."
